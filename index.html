Global weather forecasting

KAI-α: a lighter global forecaster

KAIST MetaEarth Research Center
2024–Present · In progress

An ultralight convolutional model for medium-range global weather forecasting. The research asks how much forecasting skill can be retained with a substantially smaller parameter budget.

My contributionI co-developed the model and built end-to-end training and inference pipelines, including a modular evaluation framework for comparing architectures on common benchmarks.

Approximately 8× fewer parameters than GraphCast and FourCastNet, with competitive medium-range forecasting skill in the project’s evaluations.

Graph-based climate downscaling

Zameen: learning weather through terrain

KAIST · Undergraduate Research Program
Spring 2025 · Ongoing research · Public code

Can a downscaler learn how terrain shapes local weather—and carry that relationship into a region it has never seen?

Coarse fields miss the spatial detail needed for regional analysis. Zameen reconstructs seven daily surface variables at 0.25° from 1.5° ERA5 inputs, with a graph hierarchy that connects large-scale weather to local terrain.

My contributionI built the multi-scale graph downscaler and its spectral–structural learning objective. The model conditions message passing on terrain differences and atmospheric flow, then predicts a correction to bicubic interpolation.

0.283 K temperature RMSE72.4% lower than bicubic on the 2020 test year, averaged across six fitted domains. On three unseen domains, temperature RMSE is 0.428 K—46.2% lower than bicubic.

Perfect-model ERA5 experiment. Fine-scale spectral recovery is less consistent on unseen geography.

About

I’m interested in what a model needs to know about the world to generalize. In my downscaling work, that becomes a concrete question: can relationships between neighbouring terrain features tell us more than a model’s familiarity with a particular place?

Weather and climate bring together the questions I keep coming back to in engineering: spatial structure, physical constraints, and the gap between a useful approximation and a misleading one. My studies in civil and environmental engineering and computer science give me ways to approach both the physical problem and its computational form.

I like working across the whole experiment—from the data representation to the training pipeline to the plot that reveals where the model fails. I’d welcome collaborators who care about geographic generalization, efficient forecasting, or making scientific ML results easier to inspect and reproduce.

Outside the model itself, I also care about explaining it clearly. Teaching writing and presentation is another way I work on that.
