02 / About

A bit about me

I study Civil and Environmental Engineering at KAIST, with additional coursework in Computer Science and a minor in Business and Technology Management. That combination — earth systems thinking next to deep learning and applied data work — shapes most of what I do.

My research lives at the intersection of deep learning, climate science, geospatial data, and graph neural networks. I'm especially interested in models that respect physical structure: ultralight forecasters that match much larger systems in skill, downscalers that preserve spectral properties, and graph architectures that learn over the irregular geometries of real-world climate fields.

On the engineering side, I build scalable training and inference pipelines, modular evaluation frameworks, and the tooling that turns research code into something benchmark-able.
